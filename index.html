The platform

Twelve modules behind one Microsoft sign-in

Portfolio

Every building at a glance

Occupancy, leased and trend for each property, with a badge showing whether today's data arrived.

Dashboard · Daily

The morning read

Occupancy, notices, renewals, delinquency, maintenance, leasing funnel and availability, plus a written list of what needs attention. Each user can rearrange the cards.

Dashboard · Monthly

Financials

Income statement against budget, balance sheet, cash, capex and rent collected, with drill-down to individual general-ledger lines.

Insights & Alerts

Checks on the books

Automated checks on supplier bills, AP aging, bank reconciliations, bad debt and open-ended concessions, with acknowledge and resolve.

KPIs

Property-manager scorecard

Leasing, renewal, collections, turnover and staffing KPIs by building and month, with the reason behind any gap.

Pricing Tool

A price for every unit

Price at for each vacant and expiring unit, Hold above for renewals, the competitive set, and Excel and PDF pricing memos.

Track Record

Every price, graded

Each recommendation compared with the rent the unit actually leased at, by building, bedroom count and model.

Document Center

Monthly reports, filed

Statement packages arrive by email and SharePoint and are filed by building and month without anyone touching them.

Ask

An assistant over all of it

Plain-English questions over every screen and every ledger line, answered only from what that person may see, with web research and Excel export.

Data

Feeds and models

Feed health, comp lists, manual uploads, and model training with a gate that blocks any model worse than the live one.

Admin

Access and roles

Four roles, per-person buildings and tabs, outside guests from the management company, and "View as" to see exactly what a user sees.

Help

Tour, FAQ and feedback

A first-sign-in tour built from each user's role and tabs, common questions, and a feedback button that reaches me with the tab the user was on.

The pricing engine

A model sets the starting point; leasing speed moves it each week

Step 1 · Inputs

History and the market

Every signed lease since 2018, each unit's floor plan and features, and this week's competitor rents from the market survey, or Apartments.com when the survey is stale.

Step 2 · Model

Suggested asking rent

A gradient-boosted model predicts the asking rent that units like this one leased at within the goal, without a price cut.

Step 3 · Weekly correction

Faster or slower than the goal

Up when the floor plan leases faster than the building's goal (default: leased within 21 days), down when slower, by at most 2% a week. Units empty over 60 days can drop twice as fast, over 120 days three times.

Result

Price at

One number to post, with the reason under it. For renewals, Hold above is Price at minus the cost of a turn, spread over the building's average stay.

The model never sees a unit's posted asking rent. If it could, it would learn to copy the answer, and Track Record couldn't tell whether the pricing is any good.

Run like production software

How it stays correct, fast and safe

Continuous integration and deployment

Every push runs the automated test suite in Azure DevOps. One Docker image ships the web app and all eight jobs together.

Model governance

Champion and challenger: monthly retrains must beat the live model on a rolling backtest, and every live price is graded afterwards.

Data you can trust

Feed-health checks, a daily failure email and data-quality rules on every rent roll. A missing number shows as a dash, never a zero.

Access and privacy

Microsoft sign-in, secrets in Key Vault, per-person access to buildings and tabs, and resident names kept off shared screens.

Performance

Caching, database indexes and query reduction, driven by measurements of production round trips.

A tight feedback loop

In-app feedback goes straight to me, and most weekly releases ship a batch of user requests.
